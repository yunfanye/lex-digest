Georges St-Pierre explains the science and psychology of fighting, including training, fear, strategy, athletic longevity, and the distinction between winning regulated MMA bouts and surviving uncontrolled violence outside the cage.

# Summary

He describes fear and competition as difficult parts of the profession while expressing lasting enthusiasm for technique, conditioning, and the study of combat. Skill work, strength, endurance, weight management, recovery, injury risk, and opponent preparation all compete for limited time and physiological capacity.

Rules, weight classes, referees, known opponents, and a controlled environment make MMA a specialized contest rather than a complete model of self-defense. St-Pierre is candid about anxiety before fights, showing that courage at the highest level often means acting effectively while fear remains present.
