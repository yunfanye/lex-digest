# One-liner

Charles Isbell and Michael Littman debate machine learning as computation, statistics, and software engineering, then turn to education and what students should learn when the field's tools change faster than a university curriculum.

# Summary

Statistics emphasizes inference and uncertainty, computer science emphasizes algorithms and computation, and software practice emphasizes systems that actually run; modern ML inherits all three. The guests favor concepts such as generalization, optimization, representation, sequential decision-making, and experimental reasoning over chasing whichever library is currently popular.

They defend assignments that force students to confront ambiguity, debug their own assumptions, and learn how to make progress without a prescribed sequence of steps. Tools can widen access and automate parts of practice, while mentorship, motivation, judgment, and the design of a coherent learning experience remain human responsibilities.
