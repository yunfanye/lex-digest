# One-liner

Nic Carter explains Bitcoin's conservative design culture, the blocksize wars, settlement layers, irreversibility, custody, and scaling, arguing that a slow-changing base protocol can support faster systems above it without sacrificing the properties users value most.

# Summary

Carter describes a layered future where banks, exchanges, Lightning-like systems, or other services handle frequent transactions while the chain anchors final ownership. A merchant can receive payment without fear of a later chargeback, while a user who sends funds to the wrong address or loses keys has no central authority that can undo the mistake.

Competing factions disagreed about throughput, node costs, and what Bitcoin should become, eventually producing forks and clarifying how much influence developers, miners, businesses, and users each possessed. Carter values predictability because people hold Bitcoin partly on the expectation that supply and validation rules will not be altered whenever a powerful constituency wants a new policy.
