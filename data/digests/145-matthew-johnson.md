# One-liner

Matthew Johnson reviews the modern science of psychedelics, separating receptor pharmacology, subjective experience, addiction research, therapeutic hypotheses, and philosophical speculation while repeatedly insisting on the limits of current evidence.

# Summary

Johnson describes changes in perception, self-modeling, emotion, and communication among brain systems that make the drug class scientifically distinct from ordinary sedatives or stimulants. Trials with smokers and other patients study whether a carefully supported psychedelic session can alter entrenched patterns of reward, motivation, and self-understanding.

Expectations, preparation, supervision, and the environment can shape both acute experience and risk, so rigorous studies have to treat context as a real variable. Johnson is careful to distinguish what a participant feels about consciousness, God, or reality from what controlled scientific evidence can establish about those claims.
