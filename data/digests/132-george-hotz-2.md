# One-liner

George Hotz explains Comma AI's bet on learning-based autonomous driving, arguing that enough real driving data, a strong training loop, and a compact end-to-end system can beat stacks built from many separately engineered modules.

# Summary

The system is designed to imitate and improve from large collections of human driving rather than encode every traffic situation as an explicit hand-written rule. Hotz emphasizes collecting the situations the model gets wrong, feeding them back into training, and measuring whether the next model behaves better on the road.

The conversation is skeptical of beautiful simulated worlds that miss the messy statistical details of real driving, especially rare interactions with human road users. He repeatedly favors systems that learn a compact policy from experience over architectures whose intelligence is distributed across many manually specified components.
