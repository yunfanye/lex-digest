# One-liner

Dan Kokotov explains how speech-recognition systems turn messy audio into text, covering acoustic modeling, language models, data, accents, translation, and the engineering needed to make voice interfaces reliable outside laboratory conditions.

# Summary

A recognizer must infer likely words from acoustics while using context to resolve sounds that are ambiguous or nearly identical. Microphones, background noise, dialect, speaking style, and vocabulary all shift the input distribution, so broad deployment requires broad and carefully measured data.

Modern systems can learn larger portions of speech recognition jointly, while latency, compute, rare words, and error analysis remain concrete production problems. The conversation looks beyond transcription toward systems that understand spoken intent, cross language boundaries, and interact with software in real time.
