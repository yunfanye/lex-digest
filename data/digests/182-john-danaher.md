# One-liner

John Danaher presents jiu jitsu as a system that can be studied, decomposed, and taught, moving from body types and positional control to repetition, competition, mastery, and the challenge of turning explicit rules into fluid behavior.

# Summary

Danaher argues that the sport has no single dominant physique; mechanical advantage, reach, compactness, speed, strength, and flexibility can each support different games. Rather than collecting hundreds of isolated moves, a student can organize training around recurring positions, dilemmas, and transitions that connect into a system.

Doing the same movement many times builds automaticity, but Danaher's method also asks the student to understand why grips, angles, and reactions make the movement work. Explicit decision trees help organize knowledge, while live grappling eventually demands recognition and adaptation too fast for conscious step-by-step reasoning.
