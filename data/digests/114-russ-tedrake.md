# One-liner

Russ Tedrake explains why robots remain clumsy outside carefully engineered settings, using control theory, underactuated systems, contact, simulation, and learning to show where real-world manipulation still defeats elegant models.

# Summary

Many robots and natural systems have fewer direct actuators than degrees of freedom, so successful control must exploit dynamics instead of commanding every motion independently. Walking and manipulation involve collisions, friction, changing constraints, and discontinuities that make both simulation and optimization much harder than smooth free-space motion.

Tedrake favors combining physical structure with optimization and learning so a robot can reason from known dynamics while adapting to errors that the model misses. A domestic robot must handle clutter, deformable objects, people, uncertain geometry, and thousands of tasks that each look easy to a human but collectively defeat brittle systems.
