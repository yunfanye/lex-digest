# One-liner

Ben Goertzel surveys his approach to AGI through OpenCog and SingularityNET, arguing for systems that combine many cognitive processes instead of betting the whole problem of intelligence on a single learning method.

# Summary

Goertzel describes AGI as an architecture that brings reasoning, memory, learning, attention, and goal pursuit together rather than expecting one algorithm to supply every capability. The project reflects his belief that useful intelligence comes from the interaction of symbolic and statistical components operating over shared knowledge.

SingularityNET is presented as a way for AI services and developers to interact without one company controlling the entire marketplace or the resulting intelligence network. The conversation uses biological systems to ask how many local processes can produce coherent adaptation, a theme Goertzel sees as relevant to building artificial minds.
