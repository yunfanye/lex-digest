# One-liner

Omar Suleiman presents a Palestinian-American Muslim perspective on Gaza that joins personal grief, moral theology, political criticism, nonviolent organizing, and a determination to preserve hope without minimizing civilian suffering.

# Summary

Omar Suleiman is a Palestinian-American imam, civil-rights leader, and president of the Yaqeen Institute. His second appearance shifts from Islam broadly to Gaza after October 7. The episode is intentionally perspective-driven: Suleiman speaks from religious conviction, family and diaspora experience, activism, and grief. His interpretations of military conduct, political intent, and history are contested and should be read as his case rather than neutral adjudication.

He condemns the killing of civilians while arguing that Palestinian life and dispossession are routinely rendered invisible before and after spectacular violence. Stories of journalists, children, families, hostages, prisoners, and refugees make the moral cost concrete. He criticizes Israeli leadership and U.S. policy, supports a ceasefire, and rejects reducing Palestinian dignity or resistance to endorsement of Hamas.

The conversation examines violence and nonviolence through Martin Luther King Jr., Malcolm X, protest, occupation, and asymmetric power. Suleiman emphasizes disciplined mass action, moral witness, and political pressure while acknowledging rage and the difficulty of asking an oppressed population for saintly behavior. The analytical challenge is protecting civilians and creating political agency without rewarding attacks on civilians or treating indefinite domination as stability.

The final sections return to Muhammad and Jesus, Al-Aqsa Mosque, Ramadan, prayer, and hope. Hope is not a forecast that events will improve quickly; it is a practice that prevents grief from becoming dehumanization. Pair this testimony with Israeli and historical perspectives, but do not skip it: understanding conflict requires hearing how policies are experienced by those who bear them.

# Takeaways

## Perspective is evidence of experience, not total proof

Suleiman's account reveals what many Palestinians see, fear, and mourn. It remains distinct from independently verified claims about intent and disputed events.

## Selective empathy distorts moral reasoning

A framework that recognizes one population's civilians, hostages, or trauma while abstracting the other's will produce biased policy.

## Condemning terrorism does not settle the political question

October 7 demands judgment and accountability, but does not answer questions about occupation, blockade, settlements, statehood, refugees, or proportional response.

## Nonviolence requires organization and use

Moral appeal alone rarely changes entrenched power. Effective movements combine discipline, broad participation, economic or political pressure, and a plausible demand.

## Religious language can humanize or mobilize conflict

Sacred identity deepens belonging and resilience but can make compromise feel like betrayal. Suleiman emphasizes faith as a source of mercy and patience.

## Hope can be a duty rather than a forecast

For people living through catastrophe, hope may mean continuing to serve, testify, pray, and organize when optimism is unavailable.
