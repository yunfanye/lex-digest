# One-liner

Grant Sanderson explains the craft behind 3Blue1Brown, where mathematical exposition is designed around visual intuition, animation, narrative pacing, and the difficult choice of what a learner should see before formal notation arrives.

# Summary

Sanderson uses animation to make transformations, geometry, and dependence visible over time, especially when a static equation hides the mechanism a learner needs to notice. The goal is not to replace proof with pictures; a visual story can give the learner a mental object that later definitions and derivations make precise.

The animation system reflects repeated production problems from 3Blue1Brown videos, turning mathematical objects and transformations into programmable pieces that can be revised as the explanation changes. Sanderson emphasizes that the order of examples, questions, surprises, and definitions can determine whether a difficult concept feels inevitable or arbitrary.
