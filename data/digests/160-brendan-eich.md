# One-liner

Brendan Eich recounts the creation and evolution of JavaScript, the browser wars, Mozilla, Firefox, and Brave, explaining how web compatibility turns early language decisions into permanent constraints and how browser power shapes internet privacy.

# Summary

Eich describes mistakes made under severe time pressure that could not later be removed because too much of the web depended on existing behavior. ES6 and later JavaScript absorbed ideas from competing styles and frameworks, reflecting negotiation among implementers, standards groups, and millions of existing developers.

Rendering, privacy rules, extension APIs, advertising behavior, and standards support all change what websites can do, giving browser vendors influence far beyond interface design. Eich presents Brave's blocking and privacy choices as a response to tracking-heavy web economics while still searching for ways publishers and creators can be paid.
