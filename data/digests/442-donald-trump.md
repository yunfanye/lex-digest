# Takeaways

## Campaign rhetoric is evidence of framing

An interview reveals which identities, threats, accomplishments, and contrasts a candidate wants voters to remember. It does not by itself verify the underlying claim.

## Strength needs an operational definition

Deterrence may depend on capability, credibility, alliances, communication, and restraint, not only unpredictability or personal dominance.

## Peace promises require terms

A claim that a war can be ended quickly should specify concessions, sovereignty, guarantees, enforcement, and what happens if a party refuses.

## Election claims need evidence proportional to consequence

Allegations that undermine a democratic result require specific, verifiable proof and engagement with contrary rulings and audits.

## Personnel and implementation matter more than disavowal

A candidate's relationship to an external policy agenda is best judged through staffing, adopted proposals, actions, and documented coordination.

## A friendly interview should be read as a primary source

It can clarify voice and priorities while leaving factual disputes unresolved. Pair it with policy documents, records, and adversarial questioning.
