# One-liner

Dan Carlin brings the sensibility of Hardcore History to a conversation about war, Hitler, Alexander the Great, nuclear weapons, and the difficulty of imagining what historical extremes felt like to ordinary people living through them.

# Summary

Carlin is interested in what people knew at the time, before later outcomes made events look inevitable and simplified the choices leaders and citizens actually faced. The conversation examines figures such as Hitler and Alexander without reducing history to personality, asking how institutions, technology, ideology, and crisis amplified individual ambition.

Carlin repeatedly returns to fear, loyalty, scarcity, and violence to explain why decisions made in wartime cannot be understood only through calm peacetime intuitions. Once states can destroy cities or civilization within hours, deterrence becomes a strange political system built on credible threats that everyone hopes will never be executed.
