# One-liner

Scott Aaronson returns to computational complexity and consciousness, using P, NP, #P, quantum computing, prediction, and physical limits to separate what computation can do in principle from what it can do efficiently.

# Summary

Two problems can both be solvable in principle while differing so drastically in time or memory that one is practical and the other effectively unreachable. The famous problem asks whether every solution that can be verified efficiently can also be found efficiently, with consequences across optimization, cryptography, and mathematics.

Aaronson uses counting complexity to show that knowing how many solutions exist can demand substantially more computation than verifying a single candidate. The conversation resists treating computational sophistication as automatic proof of subjective experience, even while asking whether physical and informational constraints can narrow possible theories of mind.
