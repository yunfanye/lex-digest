# One-liner

Richard Craib explains Numerai's unusual hedge-fund model, where anonymous data scientists submit predictions on obfuscated market data and economic incentives are used to combine many weak signals into a trading system.

# Summary

Participants receive transformed features and targets, which lets the tournament test predictive methods without handing every competitor the firm's proprietary dataset. Craib wants signals whose errors are not all correlated, because combining diverse models can improve the stability of the final forecast.

The Numerai system asks participants to put economic weight behind predictions, creating a mechanism that can penalize confidently bad contributions rather than rewarding leaderboard performance alone. A trading model must survive changing regimes, transaction constraints, adversarial competition, and the fact that a pattern can disappear after enough capital notices it.
