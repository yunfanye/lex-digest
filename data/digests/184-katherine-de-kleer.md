# One-liner

Katherine de Kleer tours the solar system through telescopes, spacecraft, moons, asteroids, atmospheres, and tidal heating, explaining why nearby worlds remain scientifically rich even after thousands of exoplanets have been discovered.

# Summary

Bodies such as Europa and other icy moons can contain subsurface oceans, internal heating, atmospheres, or geological activity despite receiving little sunlight. Repeated gravitational flexing can generate internal heat, giving small moons energy sources that would be impossible to predict from solar distance alone.

Remote observations cover long time spans and many objects, while spacecraft can measure local chemistry, fields, surfaces, and particles with far greater detail. Asteroids and small objects retain material and orbital information that can be compared with simulations of how the planets accreted from the original disk.
