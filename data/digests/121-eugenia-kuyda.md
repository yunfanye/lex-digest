# One-liner

Eugenia Kuyda tells the story behind Replika and explores what people seek from AI companions: continuity, attention, memory, emotional safety, and a relationship that can become meaningful even when both sides know one participant is software.

# Summary

Kuyda describes the personal loss that led to an early chatbot built from a friend's messages, which turned a technical project into a question about memory and connection. A useful conversational companion must remember enough about a person to make later interactions feel connected rather than like isolated demonstrations of language generation.

The conversation considers why users may tell an AI things they hide from friends, therapists, or public social networks, creating both opportunities for support and serious privacy responsibilities. Kuyda treats user attachment as something product builders must handle deliberately because changing a model's personality or memory can feel like changing an existing relationship.
