# One-liner

Jordan Ellenberg explores geometry, high-dimensional spaces, proof, visualization, infinity, and mathematical culture, arguing that mathematics gives people tools to reason rigorously about objects they cannot literally picture.

# Summary

A picture can make a structure intuitive and suggest why a claim should be true, while proof determines whether the claim survives every case covered by its assumptions. Mathematicians use coordinates, symmetries, projections, algebra, and analogy to reason about spaces whose full geometry cannot be held in ordinary visual imagination.

Ellenberg discusses how infinite objects simplify and unify mathematics while creating intuitions that should not be transferred casually to finite physical systems. Definitions, examples, proofs, exposition, and standards of taste are negotiated among researchers, which influences which questions become central and which explanations endure.
