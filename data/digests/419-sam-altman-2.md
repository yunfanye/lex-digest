# Takeaways

## Governance failure is a product risk

A frontier lab's board, incentives, succession, and crisis procedures affect safety and continuity. Governance cannot be treated as paperwork outside the technical mission.

## Model progress is a stack

Capability depends on architecture, data, compute, training methods, tools, evaluation, and deployment feedback. A single scaling curve does not explain the whole system.

## Memory creates value and asymmetric information

Persistent context can make an assistant dramatically more useful. Users need understandable controls for what is stored, inferred, shared, exported, and deleted.

## Compute strategy is energy and industrial policy

Training and serving frontier models depends on chips, electricity, cooling, financing, supply chains, and permitting. Infrastructure choices shape who can compete.

## Iterative deployment needs credible boundaries

Learning from real use can reveal risks, but some failures are too costly to discover only after release. Capability thresholds and predeployment evaluations remain necessary.

## Trust requires evidence beyond personality

A persuasive CEO can communicate intent, but legitimacy comes from durable institutions, external scrutiny, clear metrics, and conduct during conflict.
