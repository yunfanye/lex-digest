# One-liner

Cal Newport explains deep work as a response to an attention economy built around email, messaging, and social media, arguing that valuable knowledge work requires long periods where the brain is allowed to stay on one hard problem.

# Summary

Newport argues that sustained concentration produces learning and output that fragmented schedules struggle to match, especially for technical and creative work. Email and chat make asking a question cheap for the sender while distributing interruptions, context switches, and follow-up obligations across everyone else.

A social-media break is useful when the recovered time is assigned to relationships, craft, exercise, reading, or other activities that meet the need the platform previously served. Newport argues that filling every idle moment with stimulation teaches the brain to expect novelty, making it harder to remain with a difficult task when rewards arrive slowly.
