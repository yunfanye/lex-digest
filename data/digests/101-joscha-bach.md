# One-liner

Joscha Bach develops a computational picture of mind in which perception is constructed, selves are models, learning discovers useful programs, and consciousness emerges from a system representing its own attention and activity.

# Summary

Bach treats experience as a model generated by the mind rather than a direct copy of the outside world, which makes perception inseparable from inference. The discussion frames learning as a search for compact procedures that predict, explain, or control experience, connecting cognition to computation rather than to a list of hand-coded rules.

Bach argues that a self-model helps organize goals, attention, and social interaction without requiring a separate metaphysical entity behind the computation. When the conversation turns to artificial general intelligence, the emphasis is on what objectives an intelligent system acquires, how it models the world, and whether its incentives remain compatible with human survival.
