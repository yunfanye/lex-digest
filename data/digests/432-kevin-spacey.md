The most durable material concerns acting and power. Mentorship can transmit exquisite craft, but fame changes how invitations, flirtation, and feedback are experienced. A person with status carries additional responsibility to notice ambiguity and create conditions where refusal has no professional cost.

# Takeaways

## Direct address creates complicity

Spacey's work in Richard III and House of Cards shows how looking into the audience can turn viewers into confidants, making moral transgression feel intimate before its cost becomes clear.

## Great acting is relational

Technique matters, but performance also depends on listening, timing, the director's frame, scene partners, and an awareness of what the audience already understands.

## Specific verdicts should remain specific

Acquittal or non-liability in a case matters and deserves accurate reporting. It should not be expanded automatically into a finding about every allegation or interaction.

## Power changes the meaning of ambiguity

Fame and professional influence can make another person's apparent participation difficult to interpret. The higher-power person should seek clear consent and remove career consequences.

## Accountability is more than self-description

Acknowledging boundaries were crossed is a beginning. Repair also depends on listening to harmed people, changed conduct, appropriate consequences, and evidence over time.

## Craft and character can be evaluated separately

A person's artistic insight can remain useful without requiring a listener to adopt the person's account of contested conduct or grant renewed trust.
