# One-liner

Bryan Johnson explains Kernel's attempt to build practical noninvasive brain interfaces, covering measurement physics, product strategy, data ownership, neuroscience, and the longer-term ambition of making the brain measurable at consumer scale.

# Summary

A useful device needs signals that are informative, repeatable, fast enough, and obtainable without making the user accept the risks and cost of surgery. Johnson describes the challenge of building hardware when neither the final application nor the route to a mass market is obvious at the company's founding.

Kernel's product design gives users explicit control over recorded data, reflecting the concern that neural measurements could reveal states people do not want employers, platforms, or insurers to own. The near-term promise is repeated human data at scale for research and health applications, while many popular visions of direct thought decoding remain much harder.
